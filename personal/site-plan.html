PixelShelf

LEVEL 01 // SITE NAME

PixelShelf

The name pairs two ideas the project rests on: pixel, a nod to gaming's visual heritage across every era and platform the library covers, and shelf, the familiar image of a library or collection where each game has its place, in and out of case, alphabetized. Together they describe exactly what the site does — it's a shelf, rendered in pixels.

Optional domain: pixelshelf.gg available*

*Availability not verified for this class exercise.

LEVEL 02 // SITE PURPOSE

Site Purpose

PixelShelf is an informational hub where visitors browse, search, and filter a curated collection of video games spanning genres and platforms. Each entry surfaces the details that matter when deciding what to play next — title, genre, platform, release year, developer, rating, and a short description — with deeper detail available through an interactive modal dialog. Game data loads dynamically from a local JSON file, and visitors can save titles that catch their eye to a personal favorites list stored in the browser, no account required.

LEVEL 03 // SCENARIOS

Scenarios

Questions a visitor might bring to the site:

  1. QUEST 1

    "I loved a platformer on the SNES years ago — what other games in that genre and era does the library have?"

  2. QUEST 2

    "I want to compare a few RPGs across different platforms before deciding what to play next — can I see them side by side?"

  3. QUEST 3

    "How do I keep a shortlist of games I'm interested in without creating an account?"

LEVEL 05 // TYPOGRAPHY

Typography

Three faces, three jobs:

Display — Press Start 2P

PixelShelf

Used only for the site name and section headings — a pixel typeface reads as a display accent here, not body copy, since it's slow to read at length.

Body — Space Grotesk

Browse the collection, search by genre or platform, and open any title to see its full detail card.

Used for all paragraph text, list content, and descriptions — a clean geometric sans that stays readable at length.

Utility — JetBrains Mono

RATING 4.5 / PLATFORM SNES / YEAR 1994

Used for data-like fragments — hex codes, quest tags, game stats — echoing the HUD text of an in-game menu.
